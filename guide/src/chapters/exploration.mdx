The world is enormous and mostly unvisited. Here's how to find your way around it.

## The Wayfinder

Sneak and right-click a **cartography table** with a compass in your hand. Choose a structure, or **a land**: any biome the world you're standing in can grow, whether you've found it or not. Pay in XP and the compass becomes a **Wayfinder** that points to the nearest one. While you hold it, the distance shows above your hotbar.

<ExplorationFacts />

Lands you haven't found yet are marked **✧ new** and shown a little dimmer, with the same hint your Atlas gives, so the list teases rather than spoils. They cost double, so a trip back home stays cheap. Terralith's biomes are in the list too, and only the ones your current dimension can grow.

Walking into the biome is what counts as finding it, not pointing at it: the Atlas still means you went there.

Every price is in **XP points**, so it's the same at any level. The screen shows it as, say, *27 XP · about 2 of your levels*: that's what it takes off your bar. At level 5 that's two levels; at level 40 it's a sliver of one.

If nothing is found in range, you're told "None within 3,200 blocks", nothing is spent, and the needle still needs its few minutes to settle before the next search.

## The Guardian's Chart

From the same table: a chart that points to the nearest structure whose **guardian** you haven't beaten yet. Beat them all and it stays blank.

Free pointings from the Explorer's Compass curio or the shop are kept until you use them, so closing the screen never wastes one.

## Safe surface

Once the first Ender Dragon falls, hostile mobs stop spawning on the Overworld's open surface. Caves, the Nether and the End stay as dangerous as ever, and bosses still roam.

## Beacon kindling

Bought in the shop: drop it on a lit campfire and it burns as a tall signal, and before long a wandering trader comes to see what the fuss is about.
