You've walked into a world that looks like the one you know, and mostly is. Same creepers, same caves, same long nights. But someone has been keeping notes, and the notes say the world keeps score now.

Wayfarer is the server's own plugin. It doesn't change how Minecraft plays; it gives you reasons to keep going once the usual goals are done. The mobs you kill are counted by family, and each family has trophies waiting at the end of its count. Bosses roam the wilds and guard the old structures. Rare drops that used to take forever now come for certain, eventually. And there's more on the way: new metals melted in a crucible, tools that reward the dull jobs, and goals the whole server works on together.

These pages are a fellow traveller's journal. They assume you know vanilla Minecraft and only explain what's new.

## Before you set out

When you join, the server offers you its **resource pack**. Accept it. Wayfarer's alloys, relics and marks are real items with their own art, and without the pack they show up as something else entirely. Your game remembers the answer, so you'll only be asked once. If you declined by accident: on the server list, choose **Edit**, set **Server Resource Packs** to **Enabled**, and join again.

On your first visit you'll also find a book in your pack with a link back here.

## The road ahead

A normal playthrough passes four landmarks, each marked by a vanilla advancement you already know. Everything in this guide is placed somewhere along that road, so you can tell what's realistic now and what's for later.

None of it is a race. There's no end to reach and nothing you can miss for good; the counts keep counting and the trophies keep waiting. Wander off the road whenever you like. That's rather the point.

## How to read this guide

The chapters follow the road. Numbers in tables come straight from the server's settings, so they're right even when the prose is a bit behind. A chapter stamped **planned** describes something that's coming but isn't on the server yet: the shape is settled, the details may change.

Boss chapters hide their surprises behind a fold. Open them if you'd rather know; leave them shut if you'd rather find out the hard way.
